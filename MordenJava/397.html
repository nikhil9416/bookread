Java does provide the Thread.setDaemon method to control this behavior, which we discuss in the next section. 15.1.3 Other abstractions of threads: non-nested with method calls To explain why the forms of concurrency used in this chapter differ from those used in chapter 7 (parallel Stream processing and the fork/join framework), we’ll note that the forms used in chapter 7 have one special property: whenever any task (or thread) is started within a method call, the same method call waits for it to complete before returning. In other words, thread creation and the matching join() happen in a way that nests properly within the call-return nesting of method calls. This idea, called strict fork/join, is depicted in figure 15.4.
