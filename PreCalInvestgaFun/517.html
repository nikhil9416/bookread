Section 8.1 Non-Right Triangles: Laws of Sines and Cosines 507 Example 10 To measure the height of a hill, a woman measures the angle of elevation to the top of the hill to be 24 degrees. She then moves back 200 feet and measures the angle of elevation to be 22 degrees. Find the height of the hill. As with many problems of this nature, it will be helpful to draw a picture. Notice there are three triangles formed here – the right triangle including the height h and the 22 degree angle, the right triangle including the height h and the 24 degree angle, and the (non-right) obtuse triangle including the 200 ft side. Since this is the triangle we have the most information for, we will begin with it. It may seem odd to work with this triangle since it does not include the desired side h, but we don’t have enough information to work with either of the right triangles yet. We can find the obtuse angle of the triangle, since it and the angle of 24 degrees complete a straight line – a 180 degree angle. The obtuse angle must be 180° - 24° = 156°. From this, we can determine that the third angle is 2°.
